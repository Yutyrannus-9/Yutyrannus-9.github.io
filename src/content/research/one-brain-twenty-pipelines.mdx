## In plain language

Brain-imaging software turns an MRI scan into measurements, such as how large the hippocampus is or how thick the cortex is. Many free tools do this, and research papers often report numbers from just one of them. I wanted to see how much those numbers depend on which tool you pick. So I took one scan of my own brain, ran it through about twenty tools, and compared what they said. The short answer: for small structures, the choice of tool matters a lot, and a general-purpose AI model for medical images was not a substitute for brain-specific tools.

## Findings

These are method-level results. The site shows no health information about my brain (see [methods](/methods)).

1. **Tools disagree most on small structures.** The largest and smallest amygdala estimates differed by about 2.7×. For most structures, the direction of left–right asymmetry flipped between tools, so a number from a single tool needs uncertainty attached.
2. **SAM-Med3D wasn't a substitute here.** Against atlas labels after 5 prompts, it reached a Dice score of 0.82 on the brainstem but under 0.06 on the lateral ventricle, third ventricle, and caudate, where it segmented the surrounding tissue instead. On its own CT sample it reached a mean Dice of 0.68 from one prompt, which suggests the setup itself was working.
3. **A possible software issue.** In FreeSurfer 7.4.1, SynthSeg's `--parc --vol` output wrote cortical parcel volumes that sum exactly to total cerebral white matter instead of cortex. I used label-map voxel counts instead, which sum to within 1.3% of its cortex estimate. *Status: observed on one scan. I haven't called it a bug; the next step is to reproduce it on FreeSurfer's sample subject `bert`.*
4. **Normative scores depend on calibration.** With no reference data from the same scanner, the choice of proxy site shifted z-scores by a median of 0.44 SD, and by up to about 2 SD for mean cortical thickness. Single-subject normative claims need same-scanner calibration.
5. **Quality control passed.** Automated QC was clean, and the surface topology was clean (Euler number −32; the usual problem threshold is about −217).

## Engineering notes

- FreeSurfer's x86 build ran under Rosetta on an Apple-silicon laptop. Its TensorFlow-based tools only ran after exposing AVX to Rosetta (`ROSETTA_ADVERTISE_AVX=1`).

## Limitations

- n = 1: one scan of one person.
- No manual ground truth. Dice scores are measured against atlas labels, not expert tracing.
- Normative comparisons used a proxy calibration site.
- Not clinical, and not a diagnosis of anything.
